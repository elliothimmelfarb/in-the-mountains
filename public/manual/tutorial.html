Tutorial — Your First Deployment

A guided walkthrough of your first hours in the valley. Read it through once, or keep it open beside the game. There is also a step-by-step coach inside the game — press Guided Tutorial on the menu.

Two ways to learn
In-game coach: from the title screen, click Guided Tutorial for contextual tips overlaid on the real interface. This page: a narrative walkthrough you can read at your own pace. Both cover the same ground.

0. Before You Start

From the title screen you can set a valley seed (any text — it generates a unique valley) and a tour length. Leave the seed blank for a random valley, choose a shorter tour (60 days) for your first run, and press Deploy. You can read this manual at any time from the menu.

1. Arrival at the COP

You're looking at the Command view. The large panel is the topographic map of your valley: shaded relief, brown contour lines every 40 meters, the blue flag marking your combat outpost (it'll be named something like COP Vimoto), and a handful of villages with house icons, colored by their attitude toward you — green friendly, amber neutral, red hostile.

Try it: drag the map to pan, scroll to zoom. Hover anywhere to read the grid, the elevation, and the terrain type in the corner. Notice how the valley floor (the river, the fields) is hemmed in by steep ridgelines and cut by draws. That terrain will decide your fights.

Zoom into your COP. It's a real fortified outpost: a ring of HESCO wall with a single ECP gate, and inside it the TOC, barracks, aid station, armory, chow hall, a motor pool and a helicopter LZ. Your soldiers live here — a guard rotates through the fighting positions and towers on the wall, the gun crews sit on their guns, and the rest cycle between the chow hall, the barracks and the yard as the clock runs. Patrols muster in the yard and file out through the gate; that's the only way through the wire.

2. Read the Board

Spend a moment with the command bar at the top. The clock reads Day 1 · 06:00 and runs continuously; next to it are the light level and the weather — note whether AIR reads ON or NO-GO, because that decides if you can call aircraft. The five bars are your campaign health. Early on, Enemy strength is high and Stability is middling. Your job over the tour is to bend those curves. To the right are the time controls.

On the left: your Active Elements, Directives from battalion (your first two will be to establish a presence and to meet the elders), the Intel Feed, and the Command Log. On the right: the patrol planner / village panel and, below it, your Logistics.

3. The Clock & the Skip

This is the heart of it: time always moves. The game opens paused. Press Space or a speed button (1–5 = 1×…16×) to let it run. There is no "advance" button — soldiers rest, supplies burn, intel arrives, and the enemy maneuvers in real time.

Most of a deployment is waiting. That's what the ⏩ skip-to-event button (T) is for: it fast-forwards through the quiet hours and stops the instant something matters — contact, a patrol reaching its objective, a project finishing, a decision at the gate, first light. Lean on it. When rounds crack, the clock automatically drops to a readable speed.

The night belongs to him
Enemy activity — including attacks on your COP — is far likelier at night and when the valley is "hot." Pushing a patrol out after dark is a calculated risk.

4. Your First Patrol

In the right-hand Patrol Planner:

  1. Leave the mission set to Presence Patrol.
  2. Under Task Organization, click a squad header (e.g. 1st Squad) to add the whole squad. Then add the DOC from the HQ element — never patrol without a medic. The panel warns you in red if you forget.
  3. Set a movement posture in the order bar — this shapes how the squad moves. Concealed moves slow and low and threads cover (hard to spot, slow); Tactical and Patrol are the workhorse paces; Traveling/Rush are fast and take the road but are loud and exposed.
  4. Hit Draw Route, then click the map to lay waypoints out toward the nearest village. The element will path through the terrain. Keep the first one short.
  5. Press Step Off.

The element musters in the yard and kits up for a few minutes, then files out the gate and moves as a squad: a squad leader and two fire teams in a doctrinal formation — a file in close country, a wedge in the open, spread out when contact is likely — with a man on point reading the ground and the rest pulling security to the flanks and rear. Watch it on the map and in Active Elements, where you can recall it. On the objective the teams set into a 360° security halt. Press 4 or ⏩ to let time run while it patrols. Sooner or later, the valley will fight.

5. Talk to a Village

Click a village on the map. A panel shows its elder, its attitude, cooperation, hidden sympathy for the insurgents, and what it wants. Two tools win hearts:

  • Send element for Shura (KLE) — select some soldiers, then send them. They walk to the village and sit with the elder; attitude, cooperation, and intel accrue over the time they spend there.
  • CERP Projects — fund a well, school, or clinic for $5,000. But it's not instant: materials are trucked in, a contractor arrives, and a squad must keep the site secure for days while it's built, or the insurgents stall and sabotage it. Park an element on the village and let the clock run.

This is not a side activity. Over a tour, the shuras and the wells move your metrics as much as any firefight — and a cooperative village feeds you the intel that keeps your soldiers alive.

6. Contact!

There's no separate combat screen and no briefing. When your soldiers and the enemy see each other, a TROOPS IN CONTACT banner appears, the clock drops to combat speed, and the fight plays out right there on the map. Hit Space to pause and think. Every round is simulated against the terrain you've been walking.

7. Fighting the Fight

The instinct of a new commander is to fire back from where he's standing. The instinct that keeps soldiers alive is cover first.

  1. Pause (Space). Find the red enemy markers (those your men can see). Note where the fire is coming from.
  2. Select your element — drag a box around them, or Left-click individuals.
  3. Get off the X. Use the Move tool (or just Right-click) to send them to the nearest rocks, wall, or fold in the ground. Being caught in the open is what gets people killed.
  4. Return fire. Right-click an enemy to engage it, or use Suppress on a position to pin it while you maneuver.
  5. Treat the wounded. Your medic moves to casualties automatically, but you can select a downed soldier and call MEDEVAC to take them off the field — turning a likely KIA into a WIA.
  6. Bring the steel. In the Fire Support panel, pick a mortar (or CAS, weather permitting), then click the impact point. Watch the DANGER CLOSE warning — and watch for civilians.

Unpause and let it play; pause again whenever you need to reassess. Use 2/3 to speed through lulls.

8. Breaking Contact

You don't have to kill every fighter. The enemy will displace up the draws when he's hurt; once the shooting stops, the TIC banner clears, the clock frees back up, and your patrol picks its task back up and continues toward its objective or home. If you've had enough — the position is bad, you're low on ammo, you have wounded — select your element and use Withdraw to pull them to safer ground, or hit recall on the task in Active Elements to bring them back inside the wire. Casualties, ammo, and the outcome all flow into the Command Log as they happen.

9. The Long Game

The costs are real: wounded soldiers are out for days, the killed are gone for good, and your supplies are lighter. Keep patrolling, keep talking to villages, keep a project secured to completion, watch your directives' deadlines, and resupply before you run dry. Use ⏩ to skip the dead hours and play the moments that matter. The tour ends with a grade — and a roll of your dead. Play to bring them home.

10. Hard-Won Tips

  • Take the high ground. Elevation is line of sight, and line of sight is life.
  • Never bunch up. Disperse your element so one burst or RPG can't catch them all.
  • Suppress, then maneuver. Pin him with a machine gun; move on him with a team.
  • Mind the empty fields. When the civilians vanish, contact is coming. Read the atmospherics.
  • Hold your fire near people. One dead civilian undoes a month of patrols.
  • Keep your medic alive and close. He is the difference between WIA and KIA.
  • Don't chase him up the draws. That's where the next ambush is set.
  • Corroborate intel. A single ICOM intercept is a rumor, not a target.
