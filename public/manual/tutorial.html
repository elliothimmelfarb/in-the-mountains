Tutorial — Your First Deployment

A guided walkthrough of your first hours in the valley. Read it through once, or keep it open beside the game. There is also a step-by-step coach inside the game — press Guided Tutorial on the menu.

Two ways to learn
In-game coach: from the title screen, click Guided Tutorial for contextual tips overlaid on the real interface. This page: a narrative walkthrough you can read at your own pace. Both cover the same ground.

0. Before You Start

From the title screen you can set a valley seed (any text — it generates a unique valley) and a tour length. Leave the seed blank for a random valley, choose a shorter tour (60 days) for your first run, and press Deploy. You can read this manual at any time from the menu.

1. Arrival at the COP

You're looking at the Command view. The large panel is the topographic map of your valley: shaded relief, brown contour lines every 40 meters, the blue flag marking your combat outpost (it'll be named something like COP Vimoto), and a handful of villages with house icons, colored by their attitude toward you — green friendly, amber neutral, red hostile.

Try it: drag the map to pan, scroll to zoom. Hover anywhere to read the grid, the elevation, and the terrain type in the corner. Notice how the valley floor (the river, the fields) is hemmed in by steep ridgelines and cut by draws. That terrain will decide your fights.

2. Read the Board

Spend a moment with the command bar at the top. The day and phase (it likely starts at Day), the light level, and the weather — note whether AIR reads ON or NO-GO, because that decides if you can call aircraft. The five bars are your campaign health. Early on, Enemy strength is high and Stability is middling. Your job over the tour is to bend those curves.

On the left: Directives from battalion (your first two will be to establish a presence and to meet the elders), the Intel Feed, and the Command Log. On the right: patrol planning and, below it, your Logistics.

3. Your First Patrol

In the right-hand panel:

  1. Leave the mission set to Presence Patrol for now.
  2. Under Task Organization, click a squad header (e.g. 1st Squad) to add the whole squad. Then scroll down and add the DOC from the HQ element — never patrol without a medic. The selected count updates at the top.
  3. Make sure Plan Route is selected above the map (it is by default). Click the map a few times to lay waypoints from the COP out toward the nearest village and back. The route line and total distance appear as you go. Keep the first one short.
  4. Press Step Off.

Most patrols return without incident — you'll see a log entry, attitudes nudge up where you walked, and time advances. Run a couple. Sooner or later, the valley will fight.

A note on composition
A balanced element has a leader, automatic weapons (SAW/240) for a base of fire, a grenadier, and a medic. The panel warns you in red if you forgot the doc.

4. Talk to a Village

Click Inspect above the map, then click a village. A panel shows its elder, its attitude, cooperation, and hidden sympathy for the insurgents. Two tools win hearts:

  • Conduct Shura (KLE) — sit down with the elder. Attitudes and cooperation rise, and you may pick up a piece of intel. It costs a phase of the day.
  • CERP Projects — spend $5,000 of reconstruction funds on a well, a school, a clinic. Lasting goodwill, and a little less sympathy for the other side.

This is not a side activity. Over a tour, the shuras and the wells move your metrics as much as any firefight — and a cooperative village feeds you the intel that keeps your soldiers alive.

5. Advancing Time

When you've given your orders for the phase, press Advance (top-right). Time moves Dawn → Day → Dusk → Night → next day. Your soldiers rest and heal at the COP, supplies tick down, and intel arrives. Between phases you may get a decision event — a walk-in informant, a sick child at the gate, a complaint from an elder, a resupply window. There are no free choices; each trades something. Read them carefully.

The night belongs to him
Enemy activity — including attacks on your COP — is far likelier at night and when the valley is "hot." Advancing into the night with hostile villages nearby is a calculated risk.

6. Contact!

When a patrol makes contact, you'll get a briefing: the situation, your element and their weapons, the estimated enemy, and what fire support is available. Read it, then Deploy to Contact. You drop into the tactical layer — the same terrain, zoomed in, every soldier yours to command. It starts paused-ish and fast; hit Space to pause and think.

7. Fighting the Fight

The instinct of a new commander is to fire back from where he's standing. The instinct that keeps soldiers alive is cover first.

  1. Pause (Space). Find the red enemy markers (those your men can see). Note where the fire is coming from.
  2. Select your element — drag a box around them, or Left-click individuals.
  3. Get off the X. Use the Move tool (or just Right-click) to send them to the nearest rocks, wall, or fold in the ground. Being caught in the open is what gets people killed.
  4. Return fire. Right-click an enemy to engage it, or use Suppress on a position to pin it while you maneuver.
  5. Treat the wounded. Your medic moves to casualties automatically, but you can select a downed soldier and call MEDEVAC to take them off the field — turning a likely KIA into a WIA.
  6. Bring the steel. In the Fire Support panel, pick a mortar (or CAS, weather permitting), then click the impact point. Watch the DANGER CLOSE warning — and watch for civilians.

Unpause and let it play; pause again whenever you need to reassess. Use 2/3 to speed through lulls.

8. Breaking Contact

You don't have to kill every fighter. The enemy will displace up the draws when he's hurt; once the shooting stops, the engagement resolves on its own. If you've had enough — the position is bad, you're low on ammo, you have wounded to evacuate — select your element, use Withdraw to pull them to safer ground, or press End Contact to break clean. Then you'll see the After-Action Review: your killed and wounded, the enemy's, ammunition expended, and any civilian casualties.

9. The Long Game

Back at the COP, the costs are real: wounded soldiers are out for days, the killed are gone for good, and your supplies are lighter. Keep patrolling, keep talking to villages, keep an eye on your directives' deadlines, and watch the five metrics trend over weeks. The tour ends with a grade — and a roll of your dead. Play to bring them home.

10. Hard-Won Tips

  • Take the high ground. Elevation is line of sight, and line of sight is life.
  • Never bunch up. Disperse your element so one burst or RPG can't catch them all.
  • Suppress, then maneuver. Pin him with a machine gun; move on him with a team.
  • Mind the empty fields. When the civilians vanish, contact is coming. Read the atmospherics.
  • Hold your fire near people. One dead civilian undoes a month of patrols.
  • Keep your medic alive and close. He is the difference between WIA and KIA.
  • Don't chase him up the draws. That's where the next ambush is set.
  • Corroborate intel. A single ICOM intercept is a rumor, not a target.
