# Verdict — Staff graphics engineer (shipped WebGL/WebGPU renderers in browsers and games). Rubric: technical correctness (formats, filtering, precision, shadow math, DPR), per-frame cost honesty on integrated GPUs, robustness (context loss, SwiftShader headless), and whether phasing keeps the game working at every step. Ruthless about hand-waved GLSL and unstated texture memory.

## WebGL terrain core — shader architecture replacing the baked-bitmap blit — score 9
**Strengths:** The strongest proposal and the right spine. Format discipline is exemplary: R32F + NEAREST + manual 4-texelFetch bilinear sidesteps the OES_texture_float_linear hazard AND the R16F precision trap with actual arithmetic (~0.6 m half-float quantization → ±0.12 rise/run normal banding — correct), and the heightAt GLSL is a verified bit-faithful port of Terrain.elevAt (same cell-corner lattice, clamp size-1.001) — I checked the cited topo.ts lighting constants (key*1.05 + fill*0.34 + sky*0.24, shade*0.94+0.02 at :169-173) and the shader transcription is term-for-term faithful. The unlit-albedo hybrid is the correct call: I verified the hash2 claim at topo.ts:83-87 — `(h^(h>>13)) * 1274126177` does exceed 2^53 before ToInt32, so a uint GLSL port is genuinely not bit-portable, and deferring the GPU albedo bake behind a perceptual-diff gate is exactly right risk allocation. The rebaked 1024² R8 sun-visibility FBO with exponential stride (5·1.13^k spans ~4.2 km — checks out) is the only shadow design in the set whose march actually reaches the occluding ridge. Perf accounting is honest and itemized (8.3 M frags × ~14 cache-hit texelFetches, 89 MB albedo+mips computed correctly, dpr-1.5 reserve lever named). Robustness is best-in-class: alive 2D fallback keeps every --disable-gpu harness baseline valid, context-loss handlers named, Phase-1 GL-blit-parity step proves canvas stack/DPR/camera math before any lighting lands. Phasing is the best of the four — every phase shippable, screenshot-diff gated.

**Weaknesses:** The 'other axes plug in without touching the core shader' claim is overstated in two places. (1) The grade hook `col = col*u_gain + u_lift` cannot express the sky axis's saturation or per-channel white balance — the grade axis would reopen the core shader immediately. (2) The reserved cloud sampler — one tap, `texture(u_cloud, uv+u_cloudOff).r` multiplied into vis — cannot host the atmosphere axis's design (two scrolled octaves, coverage-threshold smoothstep, sun-parallax offset by per-pixel elevation, and a mottle term that multiplies AMBIENT, not direct). The shadow-bake policy keys only off the sun; the sky axis's moon (keyDir swap) has no input. No cliff/slope scalar is exposed for the atmosphere axis's wetness/snow terms. Albedo alpha semantics are unpinned (P2's river glint wants a mask there; a canvas upload gives opaque 255). Midday brightness calibration is honestly flagged but unresolved. Iris Xe estimate (2-4 ms at 8.3 Mpx with 12+ dependent fetches) is slightly optimistic — the reserve lever covers it.

**Must fix:**
- Replace the gain/lift grade hook with the sky axis's full grade() block (exposure, per-channel white balance, saturation, shadow lift) at identity defaults — gain+lift cannot express saturation/WB and the grade axis would otherwise reopen the 'sealed' core shader
- Replace the reserved 1-tap cloud sampler with a chunk-splice contract: the core shader exposes worldM, elev, altNorm, N, sunVis, direct, ambient, albedo (and a derivable cliff/slope scalar) at named insertion points, and composes axis-owned GLSL chunks — the atmosphere axis's cloudField/mottle/fog terms cannot fit through one texture tap
- Shadow-bake input becomes keyDir (sun OR moon, supplied by the sky module), not the sun — otherwise the night-relight axis forks the pass
- Pin albedo alpha-channel semantics (opaque vs river mask) before bakeAlbedo ships — RGBA8 canvas upload yields alpha=255 and deciding later forces a re-bake

## Sun, sky, and time-of-day color model — SkyState, ramps, moon, night relight, dual-canvas grade — score 7.5
**Strengths:** The solar geometry is the best-verified work in the set — I re-derived the table independently: δ=21° at φ=34.8° gives sunrise 04:58/sunset 19:02 (cosH₀=−0.2668 → H₀=105.5°), noon altitude 76.2°, noon toSun y-component +0.239 with the correctly-negated north term for the +y-south world frame. All check. The consistency contract (sun below horizon ⟺ solarLight at starlight) is proven, not asserted, and `nightFactor ≡ 1 − clamp01(solar·weatherLightMult)` with solarLight PASSED IN rather than duplicated is exactly the right anti-drift design. The weather single-fold rule (direct-kill redistributes, exposure carries weatherLightMult exactly once, probe-asserted) prevents the classic double-darkening bug before it exists. The anti-solar gibbous moon (transit 37°S at midnight — checks: 90−(34.8+18)=37.2°) with keyDir swap while both intensities ~0 is an elegant night-legibility solution. The source-atop analysis is spec-correct (blend modes composite source-over, so multiply over α=0 paints a veil — verified reasoning) and converges independently with the art-seam proposal. Phase 1 (2D-only rewire) standing alone if GL slips is good phasing. The grade() GLSL block is the right shape for the core shader to adopt.

**Weaknesses:** The shadow march is arithmetically broken: 48 fixed 10 m steps = 480 m reach, but a 780 m ridge at 20° sun altitude throws 780/tan(20°) ≈ 2.1 km of shadow. The justification ('480 m ≈ max ridge throw at alt>9°; below that the floor is fully shadowed anyway') is wrong — mid-floor points 0.5-2 km down-sun of the east ridge are exactly the pixels the design's own marquee shot (floor in cast shadow until mid-morning) depends on, and the march terminates before reaching the occluder. The dawn money shot fails as specified. Second: the grade seam at WorldView:~602 grades everything drawn before drawNightLights — I verified suppression cues, casualty cues, fire-mission reticles, and callouts all draw before :605 — so threat crescents and bleed pools get up to 0.42-alpha navy tint at night, a behavior change vs today (only terrain+contours are night-washed; units never were). The 0.5 alpha cap is damage control, not a fix. Third: the CPU-baked RGBA8 normal texture is a second source of truth and strictly worse at close zoom than the terrain core's per-fragment normals. Minor: the table's 18:56 row claims solarLight=0.20, but the linear 17→19.5 ramp gives ~0.27 there (isNight flips ~19:06) — the direction of the claim holds but the constant is wrong.

**Must fix:**
- Replace the 48×10 m fixed-stride shadow march (480 m reach) with the terrain core's exponential map-spanning march — at 20° sun altitude a 780 m ridge shadows points 2.1 km away; as written the valley floor is incorrectly LIT at the exact hours the design's own Phase-2 acceptance shot requires it shadowed
- Move the grade seam from WorldView:~602 (before drawNightLights) to after world dressing / before the tactical layer — at :602 it tints units, suppression cues, casualty cues, and callouts (all verified to draw before :605), a legibility-law violation and a silent behavior change vs today
- Drop the precomputed RGBA8 normal texture; consume the terrain core's per-fragment in-shader normals (sharper at ppm 8, no second source of truth, the texelFetches are cache-resident)
- Rerun the scratch probe before pinning constants: the table's solarLight(18:56)=0.20 entry is wrong (~0.27 on the linear ramp; isNight flips ~19:06, not 18:56) — harmless to the design but the probe must gate the declination/handoff numbers, not the table
- River glint requires a river mask in albedo.a that no axis provisions — keep it cut (already 'cut first') unless the albedo bake explicitly reserves alpha for it

## atmosphere — cloud shadows, terrain-aware valley fog, aerial perspective, 2D weather evolution — score 8.5
**Strengths:** The most format-disciplined proposal: every atmosphere texture is 8-bit UNORM (RG8/R8, LINEAR, REPEAT/CLAMP) — universally filterable, zero extension dependencies, 144 KB total, all cache-resident. The CPU-baked tileable two-channel FBM (modulo-lattice, fixed seed, Float32 source retained) vs live GLSL FBM is exactly the right SwiftShader-survival call: 2 fetches + smoothstep ≈ 6 ALU vs 100+. The local-valley-floor min-field is the genuinely clever idea of the set — it solves the rising-floor problem (1550→2000 m south) that a fixed MSL fog ceiling gets wrong, and the closed-form vertical transmittance is exact, not approximate, for an orthographic camera (correctly argued: path length through a pooled layer IS the thickness). The fogVisAt parity sampler over the SAME Float32 arrays the GPU samples, gated by a scratch parity harness (<0.02 on 64 probes), is the only rigorous answer in the set to the sprites-over-GL-fog problem. Sim-clock cloud advection via CPU integration (re-rolls bend streamlines instead of teleporting shadows) with wall-clock precip is a well-argued pause-semantics split. The 90 s low-pass on weather re-rolls kills the pop. Named legibility caps (CLOUD_DENSE_MAX=0.45, SYMBOLOGY_FOG_FLOOR=0.55) as constants-next-to-table is the legibility contract done right. Perf claims are honest and small (~40 ALU + 4 tiny fetches, ~0.5 ms dpr1).

**Weaknesses:** Its biggest risk is contractual, not technical: §0 ASSUMES the terrain shader exposes worldM/elev/altNorm/N/sunVis/direct/ambient/albedo/cliff and accepts spliced GLSL chunks — but the terrain core proposal offers only a 1-tap cloud sampler and reserved fog uniforms, and exposes no cliff scalar; the mottle term needs to multiply AMBIENT inside the core's lighting combine, which requires an insertion point the core never defines. Cloud offsets are integrated mutable render state with Δclock clamped to [0,60]: a harness --hour clock-jump produces offset = seed + wind·60 — deterministic, but path-dependent vs a played-to-that-hour run; the re-seed rule on load/jump is unspecified, which will bite golden screenshots. The 'z-order identical' claim for absorbing the veil into the shader is slightly off — today's veil draws OVER decoration (WorldView:223→229) and under villages; in-shader it drops below both (small, ≤0.18 alpha, but say so). Wetness/snow non-persistence is an honestly recorded residual. Cloud-shadow-vs-relief ambiguity in stills is acknowledged with real mitigations.

**Must fix:**
- Negotiate the splice contract with the terrain core BEFORE Phase B: named chunk insertion points, the exposed locals list (including a cliff/slope scalar or its in-chunk derivation), and the ambient-mottle composition point — the core's proposed 1-tap cloud hook cannot host this design and the conflict is currently silent
- Specify the cloud-offset re-seed rule for load/clock-jump (e.g. offset := f(seed, clock) on world load and on any non-monotonic clock change) so --hour harness goldens are stable and documented rather than an accident of the Δ-clamp
- Correct or explicitly accept the veil z-order change: today's veil draws over decoration; in-shader it moves below decoration and villages — one line in the design plus a Phase-B screenshot note

## art-seam — sprite shadows, decoration, grade coherence, contours, hero assets, ART_BIBLE contract — score 8
**Strengths:** The shadow-strip pipeline is excellent production engineering: data-cast-shadow groups stripped at manifest build (sources stay the edited artifact, per the generated-files invariant), with castHeight ⟺ group presence as BUILD ERRORS — both failure modes (double shadow / missing shadow) become compile-time. The P1-at-fixed-legacy-NW-sun phase is the single best phasing idea in the set: the entire SVG strip + runtime-shadow mechanism QAs as a pure screenshot-diff against today's baselines, fully decoupled from GL. The HESCO/ECP catch (rotated statics at draw.ts:404,445 already violate the baked-SE contract today; world-axis projection after opts.rot fixes them for free) is a genuine latent-bug find. The relief-inversion flag — a real south noon sun on a north-up map is the classic cartographic inversion condition — with the concrete 25-35% fixed NW form-light recommendation is expert-level perception knowledge the GL axes lack. The grade-seam placement (world dressing graded, units/ink/FX above — verified to match today's behavior where the wash never touched units) is the correct legibility call, and the drawCopNightLights split is a thoughtful detail. Worst-case cost is stated honestly (+1800 blits ≈ +2-4 ms) with pre-wired escape hatches (two-pass, cap, per-bucket atlas), and shadows self-gate off at night/overcast via shadowAlpha.

**Weaknesses:** It forks two things other axes own better. (1) A third sun model — δ=+15°, solar noon 12:15 — disagreeing with both other proposals (δ=20/12:00 and δ=21/12:00); its 'hand-verified' numbers are also looser (claims sunset ~19:05; δ=15/noon-12:15 actually gives ~18:58). Three suns is the worst synthesis outcome possible. (2) Its fog position ('occluding fog must live on the TOP canvas; GL fog would have qalats poking through') directly contradicts the atmosphere axis and is the weaker design — it keeps the drawWeather blobs that the atmosphere axis correctly identifies as the placeholder for the real pooled fog, and the qalat-poking objection is already answered by fogVisAt alpha fades with parity-harness rigor. (3) The 'binding parity rule' that GL grade MUST be the identical alpha-mix form ('NOT a multiply, or the canvases drift') is technically wrong-headed: terrain needs exposure/WB/saturation, which mix-toward-tint cannot express; perceptual parity via a shared table + calibration screenshots is the right contract, formula identity is not. gradeFor() returning only an rgba tint is sufficient for sprites but not for terrain. Hero-asset hours (8-15 h) are honest; the shadow-mask memory (+4-5 MB) and bake time (+60-150 ms) are accounted.

**Must fix:**
- Delete the third sun model (δ=+15°, solar noon 12:15) and consume the shared sky module's SunState/spriteShadow output — the declination/noon constants must not fork across canvases, and the sky axis's δ=21/noon-12:00 is the numerically verified one
- Drop the binding GL-must-use-alpha-mix parity mandate; replace with: both canvases read one altitude-keyed table, GL applies the full grade block, 2D applies the source-atop tint, and the 06:00/12:00/17:30/22:00 calibration screenshots (already in the plan) are the acceptance gate
- Cede fog occlusion to the atmosphere axis (floor-field GL fog + fogVisAt sprite fades, parity-harness gated); cut the drawWeather blob pooling weight and fogDensityAt — keep only the drawWeather reorder so precip draws above structures
- Ship sprite shadows default-on only after the promised shoot.mjs --fps before/after at the dense-forest worst case, with the two-pass/cap escape hatch already implemented behind a flag, not merely described

## Cross-axis conflicts
- **terrain-core vs sky/ToD vs art-seam**: Three sun models in three files with three sets of constants: lib/render/gl/sun.ts (δ=+20°, noon 12:00), lib/render/atmosphere.ts (δ=+21°, noon 12:00, + moon + colors + spriteShadow), lib/render/sun.ts (δ=+15°, noon 12:15). Sprite shadows pointing differently than ridge shadows is worse than no shadows — art-seam itself says so.
  - resolution: ONE module: the sky axis's atmosphere.ts skyState() wins — it is the only numerically verified model (table re-derived and confirmed), has the richest output (sun+moon+keyDir+grade+spriteShadow+nightFactor), and the cleanest anti-drift design (solarLight passed in, never duplicated). Terrain core consumes sunDir/keyDir as uniforms; art-seam consumes spriteShadow. δ=+21°, solar noon 12:00, pinned with the scratch-sun-probe as the gate. Owner's open question on campaign month: any δ in 15-23° works, but pin it in ONE place.
- **sky/ToD vs art-seam vs terrain-core**: Who applies the color grade, in what form, at which seam. Sky: rich in-shader grade (exposure/WB/sat/lift) + 2D source-atop tint inserted at WorldView:~602, which grades units/cues/callouts (verified: all draw before :605). Art-seam: single rgba tint via gradeFor(), seam after world dressing with units ABOVE it, plus a mandate that GL use the identical alpha-mix form. Terrain core: only a gain/lift hook, which can express neither.
  - resolution: Composite resolution: GL terrain gets the sky axis's full grade() block (gain/lift hook deleted); the 2D canvas gets the source-atop tint (both proposals independently converged on source-atop with correct spec reasoning — adopt); the seam goes where art-seam puts it (after decoration/villages/COP/weather, BEFORE markers/units/orders/FX/HUD) because that matches today's behavior and the legibility law; the alpha-mix-parity mandate is dropped in favor of one shared altitude-keyed table + the 4-hour calibration screenshot gate. Both tint tables merge into atmosphere.ts.
- **atmosphere vs art-seam vs terrain-core**: Fog ownership and the occlusion model. Atmosphere: pooled fog in the GL terrain shader (floor-field + closed-form transmittance) with sprite coherence via fogVisAt alpha fades over the same Float32 arrays. Art-seam: 'occluding fog must live on the TOP canvas' via upgraded drawWeather blobs; GL keeps only non-occluding haze. Terrain core: a simple u_fogTopElev/density/color uniform hook.
  - resolution: Atmosphere wins outright: its floor-field + exp-transmittance is physically exact for this camera, its parity harness makes the sprite-mismatch risk measurable (<0.02 on 64 probes), and its per-class floors encode legibility as named constants. Art-seam's qalats-poking-through objection is answered by the fogVisAt fades (scenery fades fully, symbology floors at 0.55). The blobs die per atmosphere's plan; the terrain core's simple fog uniforms are superseded by the spliced chunk. Keep art-seam's drawWeather reorder (precip above structures) — it is orthogonal and cheap.
- **terrain-core vs sky/ToD**: Two shadow passes with different march math, thresholds, and claimed ownership: terrain core (1024² R8 FBO, 56 exponential steps spanning the map, >0.25°/≤4 Hz, sun only) vs sky (48×10 m = 480 m reach — arithmetically insufficient, >0.5°, keyDir with sun↔moon swap, claims the policy and the GLSL while terrain claims the pass). Sky's own open question flags the file-ownership collision.
  - resolution: Terrain core owns the pass, the FBO, and the march (its exponential stride is the only one that reaches a 2 km shadow throw); sky owns the policy inputs: keyDir (the moon swap is the night feature and must survive), the rebake threshold (0.25° vs 0.5° — decide by eyeballing the shadow-line stepping at 60× warp in Phase 3), and the per-frame combine semantics (shadowStrength floor so shadowed ground keeps full ambient). One writer: lib/render/gl/terrain-gl.ts + shaders.ts belong to terrain core; sky delivers keyDir/threshold/strength through SkyState.
- **terrain-core vs atmosphere**: Integration surface for cloud shadows and fog: terrain core 'reserves' a 1-tap cloud sampler (1×1 white default) and flat fog uniforms so 'axes extend TerrainEnv, never the shader plumbing'; atmosphere's design needs two scrolled octave fetches, coverage thresholding, sun-parallax by per-pixel elevation, a mottle term on AMBIENT, and a cliff scalar — none of which fit through one tap, and atmosphere's §0 assumes a chunk-splice contract the core never offers.
  - resolution: Adopt atmosphere's chunk model: the core terrain shader is assembled from axis-owned GLSL chunks (atmosphere.glsl.ts spliced at named insertion points) with a documented locals contract (worldM, elev, altNorm, N, sunVis, direct, ambient, albedo, cliff derived from the normal's dz). The core's 1-tap hook is deleted as insufficient; its uniform-contract discipline (fixed texture units, defaulted uniforms) survives as the convention for the chunks. The lighting combine line moves to: lit = albedo*(direct*sunVis*cloudShadow + ambient*cloudMottle), then applyAtmosphere, then grade — order pinned in the contract.
- **terrain-core vs atmosphere**: Both write the altitude warm/cool + haze replacement for topo.ts:296-309: terrain core §7 as 'default parameterization' in the core shader, atmosphere §4 as its own applyAtmosphere with weather/zoom awareness. Same lines, two owners; if both land, haze double-applies — atmosphere's own named risk.
  - resolution: One owner: atmosphere owns applyAtmosphere (its version is a strict superset — same default coefficients 0.08/0.13/~10%, plus visibilityM and ppm zoomBoost). Terrain core ships those constants only as the Phase-2 defaults inside the atmosphere chunk's slot, deleted from the bake in the same commit the GL path activates (atmosphere Phase B). The bake-side deletion (topo.ts:296-309) and the shade-term deletion (169-173) are listed together in the bakeAlbedo spec so neither double-applies.
- **terrain-core vs sky/ToD vs art-seam**: Night-wash lifecycle: terrain core keeps the 2D wash through Phases 1-3 ('night regression impossible') with a u_nightMix hook; sky deletes it and relights with real moonlight; art-seam replaces it with the source-atop grade pass using today's exact constants. Three different end states for topo.ts:587-592 (verified: wash draws after contours, before decoration — so today trees/qalats render full-bright at night, as art-seam observes).
  - resolution: Sequence, don't choose: (1) terrain core lands with the wash intact on the 2D path — correct transitional state; (2) the grade/night landing (one coordinated commit: art-seam's reorder + grade pass + sky's moonlit GL relight) deletes the wash in exactly one place, replacing it with sky's moon-key lighting on terrain and the tint pass on world dressing. Sky's nightFactor ≡ 1−ambientLight() keeps drawNightLights' ≤0.25 gate bit-identical throughout. The core's u_nightMix hook is subsumed by the moon term in the combine — drop it from the contract.
- **terrain-core vs atmosphere**: Snow ownership: terrain core keeps the 68% snow cap baked into the albedo ('character survives pixel-for-pixel'); atmosphere prefers snow in-shader so the dynamic snow line (68%→40% during Snow weather) has no visible seam at the baked cap edge.
  - resolution: Keep snow baked through the core's ship (lowest porting risk, the cap is part of the tuned character); atmosphere's accumulation extra uses its own band-restricted variant (dynamic snow only in the 0.40-0.68 band below the baked cap — atmosphere explicitly supports this layout). Promote snow to full shader ownership only if the cap-edge seam is visible in the Phase-E screenshots. Same deferral logic for wetness: uWet multiplies albedo, no bake change needed.
- **sky/ToD vs terrain-core**: Normal sourcing: sky precomputes normals CPU-side into an RGBA8 texture ('8-bit normals are fine at 5 m cells'); terrain core computes them per-fragment in-shader from the height texture (bake's exact forward-difference) and explicitly rejected a normal texture as a second source of truth.
  - resolution: Terrain core wins: per-fragment normals are screen-resolution sharp at ppm 8 (a fixed 512² normal map is not), cost ~12 L2-resident texelFetches, and keep one source of truth registered to the contours drawn from the same elev array. Sky's RGBA8 normal texture is cut; its lighting combine consumes the core's normalAt().
- **atmosphere vs everyone (pause semantics)**: Only atmosphere specifies clock semantics for animated atmosphere: sim-clock integrated cloud advection (frozen at pause, racing at warp) vs wall-clock precip particles. The core says only 'paused game never flickers'; sky's grade and art-seam's shadows are pure functions of secondsOfDay (implicitly sim-clock); today's drawWeather is wall-clock phase.
  - resolution: Adopt atmosphere's split as the binding rule for all axes: world-anchored phenomena (sun, shadows, clouds, fog depth, sprite shadows, grade) follow world.secondsOfDay; screen-space ephemera (precip streaks) stay wall-clock so the UI never looks hung during pause. Add atmosphere's must-fix: a documented offset re-seed on load/clock-jump so --hour harness goldens are deterministic.

## Overall
Spine: the terrain core proposal, nearly whole — its texture/format decisions, camera math, fallback strategy, and phasing are the most technically sound in the set and everything else composes onto it. Three structural amendments to the spine before anyone writes GLSL: (1) the sun/sky/grade model moves OUT of gl/sun.ts and INTO the sky axis's atmosphere.ts as the single source (δ=+21°, noon 12:00 — the verified constants; the other two sun models are deleted); (2) the core's 'reserved hook' contract is upgraded from defaulted samplers/uniforms to atmosphere's chunk-splice model with a documented locals interface (worldM/elev/altNorm/N/sunVis/direct/ambient/albedo/cliff) and a pinned combine order: albedo × (direct·sunVis·cloudShadow + ambient·cloudMottle) → applyAtmosphere(haze, veil, pooled fog) → grade(); (3) the gain/lift grade hook is replaced by sky's full grade() block, and the shadow bake takes keyDir (sun or moon) from SkyState rather than computing its own sun.
