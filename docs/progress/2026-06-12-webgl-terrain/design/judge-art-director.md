# Verdict — Art director — Korengal on film (Restrepo, Korengal, The Outpost), military cartography. Rubric: does the valley read like Kunar aerial footage (raking dawn light, hard ridge shadows, terraced slopes, dusty haze) while the tactical map stays legible at every LOD band (ART_BIBLE legibility contract is sacred)? Realism payoff per unit effort; flag tech-demo tells.

## WebGL terrain core — shader architecture replacing the baked-bitmap blit — score 9
**Strengths:** This is the proposal that keeps the map a MAP while the light goes live. The bit-faithful elevAt port ('any lattice mismatch reads as contours sliding off the terrain') is exactly the misregistration that makes a renderer read as a tech demo instead of a chart — contours welded to relief is the cartographic soul of the thing and nobody else even names it. The shadow march is the only CORRECT one in the packet: exponential stride spanning the whole 2.56 km map means the 0700 east-wall shadow actually floods the floor — the single most documented Korengal visual. CPU-unlit-albedo preserves all 26 landcover characters pixel-for-pixel (zero risk to the hard-won forest dapple/furrows/HESCO grid); mip-chain kills strategic-zoom pan shimmer; per-fragment normals make relief SHARPER than today at tactical zoom; the 2D fallback keeps the whole headless harness ecology green; reserved hooks (cloud sampler defaulting 1×1 white, fog/grade uniforms defaulting to today's look) are exactly the hard seams a synthesis needs. Phase-1 blit-parity gate before any lighting lands is the right discipline.

**Weaknesses:** It is the substrate, not the cinema — deliberately so, but it defers everything that makes the footage FEEL like Kunar: night stays the flat navy veil through Phase 3 ('the wash stays on the 2D canvas in Phases 1-3'), haze/grade are static defaults, its lighting combine has no color story (sunColor is implicit in u_keyGain — no golden hour, no blue hour). Its §7 haze/altitude-grade block and fixed u_fogTopElev fog hook duplicate and under-specify what the atmosphere axis does better — a fixed fog-top elevation cannot pool on a floor that rises 1550→2000 m. Its sun model (δ=+20°, own sun.ts) is a third parallel sun. The midday-calibration risk it flags (real 75° noon sun is flatter than the baked NW key) is real and it only half-answers it with a brightness gate, not a relief-legibility gate.

**Must fix:**
- Replace the fixed u_fogTopElev/u_fogDensity fog hook with the atmosphere axis's interface (128² local-valley-floor texture + uFogTopAdd depth) — the proposed hook signature physically cannot represent pooled fog on the rising valley floor and would have to be re-plumbed anyway.
- Add an ambient-side cloud hook: the cloud sampler currently multiplies only the direct-vis term (ndl*vis), but Overcast needs the atmosphere axis's low-amplitude ambient mottle — under Overcast the direct term is nearly dead and the hook as specified does nothing.
- Shadow-bake pass must accept a generic keyDir (sun OR moon) from the shared sky module, not a sun-only uniform, so the night moonlight relight lands without re-plumbing the FBO pass.
- Retire lib/render/gl/sun.ts as an independent model — it becomes a re-export of the unified atmosphere module with δ pinned to +21° (the sun/sky proposal's numerically verified fit), or the project ships two suns that disagree by a degree.

## Sun, sky, and time-of-day color model — the master clock made visible — score 8.5
**Strengths:** The highest realism-per-effort in the packet, and the best ART-DIRECTED document: the verified consistency table (sunrise 04:58 / sunset 19:02 against the untouched 05:00/19:30 gameplay ramps, nightFactor ≡ 1−ambientLight asserted by a probe) turns 'consistent with the gameplay curve' from vibes into arithmetic. The golden-hour insight is genuinely brilliant: evening sun at 287–296° WNW means the dynamic light CONVERGES on the ART_BIBLE's baked NW sprite key exactly when shadows are longest and most visible — 'the baked sprite art is perpetual golden hour' reframes the worst cross-contract risk (164 un-reauthorable SVGs) as a feature, and correctly notes the only true mismatch (morning ENE) is when sprite shadows are sub-8-px. The anti-solar gibbous moon is smart design, not astronomy cosplay: up all night, low southern raking light, real cast shadows via key-swap, weather kills it like the sun, 0.12 luma floor — night terrain gets FORM instead of a veil, which is both more Restrepo and more legible. Single-fold weatherLightMult rule (exposure carries it exactly once, direct/ambient redistribution is separate) prevents the double-darkening bug nobody else even anticipates. The dawn mechanism — 'the 05:00–07:00 gameplay ramp gets a literal mechanism: ridge shadows 0.8–3.7 km long, floor in cast shadow under a bright sky until ~08:00' — is the Korengal morning, stated as physics.

**Weaknesses:** Its own marquee feature has an arithmetic defect: the shadow march is 48 fixed 10 m steps = 480 m reach, justified by '≈ max ridge throw at alt>9°; below that the floor is fully shadowed anyway' — false. At alt 9° a 780 m wall shadows the floor 4.9 km out; a point 2 km from the wall is shadowed but the 480 m march never reaches the occluder, so the dawn floor-shadow it calls the money shot gets truncated to a 480 m fringe. It also re-specifies things the terrain core does better: CPU-baked RGBA8 normal texture (8-bit quantization, fixed resolution) loses to per-fragment normals at close zoom; the grade seam at WorldView:605 (confirmed: units draw BEFORE drawNightLights) tints units — today units are never night-washed, and the legibility law plus the art-seam proposal both say keep it that way. River sun-glint assumes albedo.a carries a river mask, a cross-axis ask the terrain core never agreed to. Phase-1 interim 2D veil is throwaway work if GL is on schedule.

**Must fix:**
- Adopt the terrain core's exponential-stride whole-map shadow march; the 480 m fixed-reach march truncates dawn/dusk cast shadows at exactly the hours the proposal's own §1 calls the most documented Korengal visual. Keep this proposal's policy layer (0.5° threshold, ≤1 rebake per 2 frames, sun↔moon key swap while both intensities ≈ 0).
- Drop the CPU-baked RGBA8 normal texture; consume the terrain core's per-fragment forward-difference normals (which also match the existing bake's math, protecting the look at the parity gate).
- Move the grade seam below the units/orders/FX block per the art-seam ordering — units, labels, and ink stay ungraded as today; the 0.5 alpha cap then applies only to world dressing.
- Gate the river-glint flourish on the terrain core explicitly agreeing to encode a river mask in albedo.a; otherwise cut it — it is also the one item here with tech-demo odor (specular glint on an orthographic top-down map).

## atmosphere — cloud shadows, terrain-aware valley fog, aerial perspective, 2D weather evolution — score 9
**Strengths:** Contains the single best idea in the packet: the 128² local-valley-floor min-field. Fog depth measured above the LOCAL floor is what makes pooling real on a valley whose floor climbs 1550→2000 m — fog hugs the river draw AND tributary draws, ridges poke out of the 300 m Fog-weather deck, and the picture visually EXPLAINS airAvailable=false. That is the Korengal inversion, grounded in sim truth — the opposite of a tech demo. The cloud-scale reasoning is precisely right art direction with numbers: 1800 m base shapes → 3-6 distinct patches at strategic zoom, 'DO-NOT shrink below ~1200 m: smaller reads as sensor dirt'; per-label edge softness (crisp Clear cumulus, shapeless Rain mottle) matches how skies actually photograph. Sim-clock advection by INTEGRATION (offset += wind·Δclock) is the sharp catch — closed-form phase would teleport every shadow on each 4-9 h wind re-roll — and the pause/warp semantics (pause = still photo, 8× warp = timelapse clouds racing the terminator; precip stays wall-clock) is the cheapest 'holy shit' in scope. fogVisAt sampling the SAME Float32 arrays the GPU uses, with named per-class floors (SYMBOLOGY_FOG_FLOOR=0.55, CONTOUR_FOG_FLOOR=0.65, CLOUD_DENSE_MAX=0.45) encodes the legibility contract as compiler-visible constants. Honest rejection of radial desaturation on an orthographic camera, replaced by the ppm-driven hazeK boost — the truthful version of 'pulling out adds air'. 144 KB of texture, ~40 ALU. Cheapest axis, huge payoff.

**Weaknesses:** Cloud shadows misreading as terrain relief in stills/pause is flagged but remains its biggest aesthetic risk — a 0.34-dense patch lying across a spur in a screenshot WILL read as a reentrant to some eyes; the mitigation (caps, soft edges, motion) is plausible but needs the pause-frame review it promises. The sun-parallax deck projection (ridge tops closer to the deck) is sub-perceptual at top-down zooms — the proposal itself offers to delete it; that instinct is correct, treat it as cut-by-default-unless-visible. Two open dependencies are left dangling: weather.ceiling AGL-vs-MSL (changes both uCloudAlt and Fog depth — a wrong guess fogs the wrong half of the valley) and snow ownership. Wetness/snow non-persistence is an accepted residual, fine, but a reload mid-storm visibly popping dry ground should be named in the issues log, not just here.

**Must fix:**
- Pin weather.ceiling AGL-vs-MSL against the air-support code before Phase C — it is load-bearing for both uCloudAlt and the Fog-weather depth, and the design currently guesses.
- Coordinate the CLOUD table with the sky module's direct-kill so Overcast/Rain never double-dims (cloud dense × collapsed direct × exposure·weatherLightMult); add the Overcast row to the screenshot matrix with a mean-luma assertion against ambientLight semantics.
- Resolve snow ownership (move the baked 68% cap into the shader, as preferred) in the SAME commit that de-lights the albedo — landing them separately ships a visible seam at the baked cap edge for however long the gap lasts.

## art-seam — sprite shadows, decoration, grade coherence, contours, hero assets, ART_BIBLE contract — score 7.5
**Strengths:** The sprite-shadow mechanism is the right call at every level: projected self-silhouette (one-time half-res blurred mask, slid/stretched along the live sun axis) needs zero new art for 33 sprites, and because projection happens in world axes after opts.rot it fixes a LATENT bug it actually found — HESCO/ECP segments are drawn rotated (draw.ts:404,445) so their baked 'SE' shadows already point the wrong way around the ring today. The data-cast-shadow strip with build-time cross-validation (castHeight ⟺ wrap group, mismatches are BUILD ERRORS) is real craft. P1-at-the-legacy-NW-angle so QA is a pure screenshot diff against baseline is exactly how you de-risk hand-editing 33 SVGs. The hero list is the most Kunar-literate text in the packet — cator-and-cribbage stone-and-timber qalats, plywood B-huts with sandbagged roofs, whitewashed walnut trunks, dead-spike deodar variants, HESCO first because it is 'the most-instanced sprite at the COP; biggest win per authoring hour'. The ART_BIBLE rewrite enumerates what stays sacred (palette, LOD bands, flat-ink rule) instead of hand-waving. The relief-inversion flag (real noon sun due south on a north-up map) is a legibility hazard no other proposal names. The draw-order re-cut — world dressing graded/fogged, units/orders/ink above the grade — is the correct codification of the legibility law.

**Weaknesses:** Three of its four coherence systems are superseded by stronger versions elsewhere, and one of its binding rules is actively harmful. (a) Its private sun model (δ=+15°, solar noon 12:15) is a third divergent sun, less verified than the sun/sky proposal's table. (b) gradeFor() re-derives a 'solarLight-shaped ramp' from sun elevation — exactly the silent-drift hazard the sun/sky proposal engineered out by passing solarLight() IN. (c) Its fog plan (top-canvas blob field with a pooling weight) reuses the placeholder blobs the atmosphere axis correctly deletes; its objection to GL fog ('qalats poke through fog banks') is already answered by fogVisAt fading sprites from the same field. (d) The 'binding parity rule' that GL must grade via the identical alpha-mix form 'NOT a multiply' would lobotomize the terrain grade — no exposure, no white balance, no scotopic desaturation — sacrificing the entire cinematic range of the big canvas to protect sprite parity that a shared keyframe table plus a dusk screenshot gate achieves anyway. (e) Its night end-state is literally today's navy wash constants (gradeFor returns rgba(8,14,30,…)) — the flat veil the rest of the packet agrees must die. Worst-case +1800 shadow blits at dense-forest close zoom is acknowledged with escape hatches but unmeasured.

**Must fix:**
- Delete the private sun model and gradeFor(); consume the unified sky module's SkyState (including its spriteShadow {dx,dy,alpha} output, which already exists for exactly this consumer). δ=+15°/noon 12:15 must not survive into a merged codebase next to δ=+21°/noon 12:00.
- Drop the binding GL alpha-mix parity rule. Terrain keeps the full shader grade (exposure/WB/saturation/lift); sprites get the source-atop tint from the SAME altitude-keyed table; coherence is verified by the 06:00/17:30/22:00 dual-canvas calibration screenshots already proposed — if sprites float, the spriteTint row is tuned, not the terrain shader flattened.
- Cede fog to the atmosphere axis's fogVisAt + per-class floors; delete the blob-pooling weight plan. Keep this proposal's village-label exemption and the world-dressing-vs-ink ordering, which the atmosphere axis should adopt verbatim.
- Replace gradeFor's night branch (the navy wash constants) with the sky module's night spriteTint row — the end state is moonlit relight on the GL canvas with a capped cool tint on dressing, not the old veil reborn.
- Measure the dense-forest worst case at P1 with shoot.mjs --fps as proposed, and pre-commit to which escape hatch fires (shadow cap on first N y-sorted items vs vegetation-only 5° sun-bucket atlases) rather than deciding under regression pressure.

## Cross-axis conflicts
- **terrain-core (gl/sun.ts, δ=+20°, noon 12:00) vs sun/sky (atmosphere.ts skyState, δ=+21°, noon 12:00) vs art-seam (render/sun.ts, δ=+15°, noon 12:15)**: Three independent sun models with different declinations and solar noons. Merged naively, terrain shadows, sprite shadows, and the grade would each follow a slightly different sun — sprite shadows pointing differently than ridge shadows is worse than no shadows at all (the art-seam proposal says this itself).
  - resolution: ONE sun: the sun/sky proposal's skyState is the single source of light truth — it is the only one with a numerically verified consistency table against solarLight() and it already exports sunDir, keyDir, grade, AND spriteShadow for every consumer. Pin δ=+21.0°, solar noon 12:00, φ=34.8°N. Terrain-core's gl/sun.ts becomes a thin re-export or dies; art-seam's sunState/gradeFor die; drawWorldSprite consumes SkyState.spriteShadow. The terrain core's open question ('any δ in +15..+23 works') concedes the point — take the verified one.
- **sun/sky (grade seam before drawNightLights at WorldView:605 → grades units; rich in-shader grade: exposure/WB/sat/lift) vs art-seam (grade pass UNDER units; binding rule that GL must use the identical alpha-mix form, 'NOT a multiply')**: Two incompatible grade contracts: WHERE the 2D seam sits (over vs under units — confirmed in code: units draw before :605, so the sun/sky placement tints faction accents) and WHAT math the GL side uses (full cinematic grade vs alpha-mix lerp matching source-atop).
  - resolution: Split the difference along the legibility law: seam placement goes to art-seam (grade between world dressing and units — units/orders/ink/HUD stay ungraded, matching today's accepted behavior where units were never night-washed); grade math goes to sun/sky (terrain keeps exposure/WB/saturation/lift in-shader — that is where the Kunar cinema lives; the binding alpha-mix rule is dropped). Parity comes from both passes reading one altitude-keyed table, verified by the dusk/night dual-canvas calibration screenshots. The sun/sky 0.5 tint cap now applies only to world dressing.
- **terrain-core (night wash stays on 2D through Phase 3, u_nightMix reserved) vs sun/sky (wash dies, moonlit relight, 0.12 luma floor) vs art-seam (gradeFor night branch returns today's navy rgba(8,14,30) constants)**: Three different night end-states: deferred veil, real moonlight, and the old veil rebuilt inside the new grade pass.
  - resolution: End state is the sun/sky moonlit relight — anti-solar gibbous moon as the shadow key, scotopic grade, terrain keeps FORM at night (more Restrepo AND more legible than any veil). Terrain-core's 'wash stays until the grade axis owns night' is correct interim sequencing only; art-seam's navy gradeFor branch is replaced by the sky module's night spriteTint row. Acceptance: the night-firefight screenshot where muzzle blooms read over moonlit relief and threat crescents pass the squint test.
- **atmosphere (pooled GL fog via 128² floor-field + fogVisAt fading 2D sprites, per-class floors) vs art-seam (occluding fog must live on the TOP canvas as upgraded blob field; GL keeps only non-occluding haze) vs terrain-core (fixed u_fogTopElev/u_fogDensity hook)**: Three fog owners. Art-seam's objection — 'GL fog under the sprite layer would have qalats poking through a fog bank' — assumes sprites don't fade; terrain-core's fixed fog-top elevation cannot pool on a floor rising 1550→2000 m (it would drown the north half and never fog the south draw).
  - resolution: Atmosphere wins outright: GL fog from the local-valley-floor field, sprites faded by fogVisAt over the SAME Float32 arrays (exact parity, asserted by the scratch harness), scenery fades fully while symbology floors at 0.55. Terrain-core changes its hook signature to the floor-field texture + uFogTopAdd interface. Art-seam's blob-pooling plan is deleted; its village-label exemption and dressing-vs-ink fade classes are adopted into the atmosphere axis's per-class table.
- **terrain-core (56-step exponential-stride march spanning the whole map, 0.25° threshold, ≤4 Hz, sun-only) vs sun/sky (48×10 m fixed steps = 480 m reach, 0.5° threshold, sun↔moon key swap) vs art-seam (24-step log-march sketch)**: One shadow pass, three specifications — and the reach question is not a style choice: at sun altitude 6-10° the occluding ridge sits 2-5 km up-sun from the shadowed floor, so the 480 m fixed reach silently truncates the dawn east-wall shadow that every proposal calls the marquee shot.
  - resolution: Terrain-core owns the pass, the FBO, and the march (exponential stride to map extent — verified arithmetic above); sun/sky owns the POLICY layer: keyDir input (enabling moon shadows via key swap while both intensities are ≈0), the motion threshold (unify at 0.25-0.5°, ≤4 Hz, ≤1 rebake per 2 frames). Art-seam's GLSL sketch is dropped. One writer: lib/render/gl/ shadow code is terrain-core's file; the policy lives in the sky module and arrives as parameters.
- **terrain-core (per-fragment forward-difference normals from the R32F heightmap, matching topo.ts:155-165) vs sun/sky (CPU-precomputed RGBA8 normal texture, central-difference) vs art-seam (central-difference texelFetch sketch)**: Normal source and finite-difference scheme disagree — 8-bit baked normals quantize on gentle slopes and are resolution-capped; central vs forward difference subtly shifts the relief relative to the existing bake's look and the Phase-2 parity gate.
  - resolution: Terrain-core's per-fragment forward-difference normals win: screen-resolution relief at close zoom, zero extra texture, and bit-matched to the math the current bake uses — which is what makes the 'force the old NW key, diff against today' calibration gate meaningful. Sun/sky's normal texture and art-seam's sketch are dropped.
- **terrain-core (§7 default altitude warm/cool + haze in the core shader as defaults) vs atmosphere ('cross-axis deletion I require': topo.ts:296-309 moves into MY applyAtmosphere chunk, weather- and zoom-aware)**: Both implement the altitude grade and haze; shipped together the valley gets double-hazed and double-cooled at altitude.
  - resolution: Atmosphere owns these terms — its version is live against visibilityM and ppm, which is the whole point of un-baking them. Terrain-core ships the splice point, not the implementation: composition order pinned as albedo × light (terrain-core) → applyAtmosphere: alt warm/cool, haze, veil, pooled fog (atmosphere) → grade: exposure/WB/sat/lift (sun/sky) → out. Phase-B acceptance: neutral atmosphere defaults reproduce the baked look against HEAD before anything goes live.
- **sun/sky (WX table with per-label cloudShadow density; SkyState.cloudShadowDensity hook) vs atmosphere (CLOUD table: cover/dense/soft/mottle per label; owns uCloudTex and offsets)**: Two weather→cloud parameter tables; also two consumers of the terrain core's cloud sampler slot (which currently multiplies only the direct-vis term, while atmosphere needs an additional ambient mottle term).
  - resolution: Atmosphere owns everything cloud: the table, the tile bake, the offsets, both shader terms. SkyState keeps only what it must (sunIntensity already carries the weather direct-kill); its cloudShadowDensity field is deleted or becomes a read-through to the atmosphere table. Terrain-core adds the ambient-mottle hook next to the existing direct-vis cloud multiply. Assert no double-dimming under Overcast in the screenshot matrix (direct collapse × cloud dense × exposure must still match ambientLight semantics).
- **atmosphere (clouds/fog advect on SIM clock — frozen on pause, racing at warp; precip stays wall-clock) vs art-seam (keeps drawWeather's existing wall-clock blob field for its fog plan) vs terrain-core ('a paused game never flickers')**: Pause semantics: if any world-anchored atmospheric effect runs on wall clock while the sun and shadows freeze, the paused map animates incoherently.
  - resolution: Atmosphere's split is law for every axis: world-anchored phenomena (clouds, fog banks, shadow line, wetness/snow) follow the master clock — pause is a still photo, warp is a timelapse; screen-space ephemera (precip streaks) stay wall-clock so the UI never looks hung. Art-seam's wall-clock fog inherits the resolution by ceding fog entirely (conflict above).
- **art-seam (noon relief-inversion flag: blend 25-35% fixed NW form light into GL diffuse; sprites keep matching soft NW surface bias) vs terrain-core (sun-relative anti-solar fill only; midday calibration gate measures brightness, not relief legibility)**: Under a real 76°-altitude noon sun the map flattens and a south key on a north-up map invites relief inversion — today's permanent raking NW bake is maximum relief legibility all day, so a 'more real' noon can be a LESS readable map. No proposal fully owns this, and terrain-core's fill is sun-relative, not the fixed NW term art-seam's sprite contract assumes exists.
  - resolution: Ship the lever, decide on evidence: add uFormLightNW (fixed NW directional term blended into the diffuse, default ~0.2) to the terrain shader, and extend the Phase-2 gate from brightness-match to a NOON LEGIBILITY SQUINT TEST — 12:00 Clear at all four LOD bands must read relief no worse than today's bake. If the term lands at ~0, art-seam drops the sprites' residual NW surface bias too (its own stated fallback), so the convention stays two-sided either way.
- **sun/sky (river sun-glint reads a river mask from albedo.a) vs terrain-core (albedo is RGBA8 with alpha unspecified/opaque; GL canvas created alpha:false)**: A cross-axis data dependency (river mask in the albedo alpha channel) that the albedo owner never agreed to encode.
  - resolution: Glint is Phase-4 cut-first polish on its own proposal's terms: gate it on terrain-core explicitly agreeing to encode the river mask in albedo.a during bakeAlbedo (cheap — the bake already knows the river class). If not agreed, cut with zero mourning; an orthographic top-down specular glint is the packet's highest tech-demo-risk flourish anyway.

## Overall
SPINE: terrain-core's TerrainGL architecture is the skeleton — fullscreen-tri draw, R32F height + manual bilinear (the bit-faithful elevAt port), CPU-unlit albedo at 8 px/cell, exponential-stride shadow FBO, fixed uniform/texture contract with defaulted hooks, alive 2D fallback, and the parity-gated phase ladder (P0 overlay split → P1 GL blit parity → P2 sun unlock behind the forced-NW calibration diff). It is the only proposal whose marquee math is correct end-to-end and whose registration discipline keeps the map a map. GRAFT INTO THE SPINE, in order: (1) Sun/sky's skyState becomes the single light authority — δ=+21°, noon 12:00, the color/grade keyframe tables, the moon, nightFactor ≡ 1−ambientLight with its probe — feeding terrain-core's uniforms, the shadow pass's keyDir (march spec stays terrain-core's; policy and key swap are sun/sky's), and art-seam's sprite shadows via SkyState.spriteShadow. Terrain-core's gl/sun.ts and art-seam's sun.ts/gradeFor are deleted before they exist. (2) Atmosphere owns everything between lighting and grade: composition order pinned as albedo × light → applyAtmosphere (alt warm/cool, haze, veil, floor-field fog, cloud shadow on direct + mottle on ambient) → grade (exposure/WB/sat/lift) → out. Terrain-core's §7 default haze block and fixed fog-top hook are superseded by the atmosphere interface; the cloud slot gains an ambient-mottle term. Sim-clock advection and the 90 s low-pass are law.
