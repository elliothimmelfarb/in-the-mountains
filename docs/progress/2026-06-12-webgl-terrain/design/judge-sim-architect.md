# Verdict — Simulation architect — rubric: layer-line purity (lib/sim untouched, lib/render React-free, store.ts the only bridge), determinism (no per-frame randomness; seeded/hash/sim-clock only), save compatibility, gameplay-light sacredness (solarLight/ambientLight/isNight semantics frozen), and headless-harness verifiability (shoot.mjs / visual-matrix.mjs / smoke.ts stay green). Flags any render-state-into-sim coupling.

## WebGL terrain core (shader architecture replacing the baked-bitmap blit) — score 9
**Strengths:** The strongest engineering document of the four and the correct spine. Layer-pure: every new file is lib/render/gl/*, the store is touched only for warm phases, lib/sim untouched. The R32F+NEAREST manual-bilinear heightAt is a verified bit-faithful port of Terrain.elevAt (same cell-corner lattice, clamp size−1.001) — this is exactly what keeps the GL relief registered to the 2D contours drawn from the same array, my top-priority invariant. Its normal math correctly identifies the bake's forward difference (topo.ts:157-165 — I verified; the other two proposals miscite it as central). The unlit-albedo hybrid ('the existing bake minus its lighting terms, so all 26 landcover characters survive pixel-for-pixel') de-risks the tuned splat completely, and the honest note that JS hash2 relies on double-precision int32-coercion past 2^53 and cannot be ported bit-exactly to uint GLSL is the kind of skepticism I want — Phase 4 behind a perceptual-diff gate is right. Determinism is clean: sun from secondsOfDay, shadow rebake keyed to sun-angle threshold so a paused game never flickers. Keeping the 2D lit-bake path 'byte-identical at one branch point' preserves the entire --disable-gpu harness ecology and its baselines — the single best harness decision in the set. Perf numbers are itemized and credible. The exponential-stride whole-map shadow march (5 m·1.13^56 spans 2.56 km) is geometrically correct for 780 m ridges at low sun.

**Weaknesses:** The extension contract is too rigid for the atmosphere axis: 'Atmosphere/grade axes extend TerrainEnv, never the shader plumbing' and a u_cloud tap at 'uv + u_cloudOff' cannot express P3's elevation-parallax cloud projection, ambient mottle, or fog transmittance — P3 explicitly assumes a GLSL splice ('spliced into the terrain shader by the GL-core axis'). The sun model duplicates P2's with different constants (δ=+20° vs +21°) and no moon, so its shadow bake 'skip[s] baking when alt < 0°' — incompatible with P2's moonlit night relight, which needs a keyDir sun↔moon swap. It also retains the 2D navy wash through Phase 3, which is the right conservatism for landing but must be explicitly sequenced against P2/P4 who both delete it. The 4 Hz rebake throttle is wall-clock; harmless for screenshot determinism (the angle threshold dominates at a pinned clock) but should be stated as a perf policy, not an output input.

**Must fix:**
- Open a named GLSL splice seam (cloudShadow/cloudMottle/applyAtmosphere functions + their uniforms) for the atmosphere axis — the 1×1-white u_cloud tap and 'never the shader plumbing' contract cannot host P3's elev-parallax clouds, ambient mottle, or pooled-fog transmittance; keep uniforms-only for the grade axis
- Shadow-bake pass must accept an external keyDir (sun OR moon) from the sky module instead of skipping when sun alt < 0° — P2's night relight depends on moon-key cast shadows through the same pass
- Delete lib/render/gl/sun.ts as a separate model; consume the single sky module's sunDir/keyDir (constants unified on P2's verified δ=+21° table) so two suns can never drift

## Sun, sky, and time-of-day color model (SkyState API, ramps, night relight, dual-canvas grade) — score 8.5
**Strengths:** Owns the sacredness rubric outright. nightFactor ≡ 1 − clamp01(solar × weatherLightMult) with solarLight() PASSED IN ('the gameplay curve is never duplicated in the render layer') is the best single idea in the set — the visual model is parameterized by the sacred curve and can never disagree with it; weatherLightMult is genuinely exported at campaign.ts:186 so the import is legal and read-only. The consistency proof is computed, not vibed: the verified table (sunrise 04:58 / sunset 19:02 vs ramps 05:00/19:30, isNight flip at +1.5°/+6° altitude) plus scratch-sun-probe asserting the identity across 24 h × 6 weathers is exactly my verification culture. The source-atop rationale is verified against the compositing spec ('where dest α=0, Cr=Cs' kills multiply/soft-light) — load-bearing and correct for a transparent-backed top canvas. The weather single-fold rule (exposure carries weatherLightMult exactly once; direct/ambient redistribution is separate) prevents the classic double-darkening bug. Moon-as-anti-solar-gibbous with key-swap during near-zero intensity is elegant; Phase 1 standing alone pre-GL keeps the game never hostage to the underlayer. The sign-flip discipline (+y is SOUTH, noon vector (0,+0.239,0.971)) is checked against physical anchors.

**Weaknesses:** Two technical conflicts with the terrain core it must yield on. (1) It proposes CPU-precomputed RGBA8 normals ('same central-difference as topo.ts:157-165' — miscited; the code is a forward difference) where P1 computes per-fragment normals from the height texture; a normal texture is a second source of truth and is strictly worse at close zoom — P1 rejected it for stated reasons. (2) Its shadow march is 48 linear steps × 10 m = 480 m reach, justified by 'below that the floor is fully shadowed anyway' — unsupported: a 780 m ridge at 9° sun altitude throws ~4.9 km; P1's exponential stride spans the whole map for the same fetch budget. SkyState also scope-creeps into other axes (cloudShadowDensity, fogColor, spriteShadow) — fine as hooks, but ownership must be assigned or two modules will compute fog color. Open question it raises itself: who owns shadow-pass.ts — must be settled (P1 owns the pass, P2 owns the policy).

**Must fix:**
- Drop the precomputed RGBA8 normal texture; consume the terrain core's in-shader per-fragment normalAt (forward-difference per topo.ts:157-165 as verified) — one source of truth, sharper at ppm 8
- Replace the 480 m-reach linear shadow march with the terrain core's exponential-stride whole-map march — 480 m demonstrably truncates real ridge shadows at low sun (780 m ridge at 9° alt throws ~4.9 km); keep this axis's refresh policy (threshold, key-swap, per-2-frame cap) on top of that pass
- Reconcile the declination with the terrain core's sun model into ONE module (δ=+21°, solar noon 12:00, per this proposal's verified table) — three independent sun models across the proposals is the synthesis's biggest drift hazard

## Atmosphere (cloud shadows, valley fog pooling, aerial perspective, 2D weather evolution) — score 8.5
**Strengths:** The best determinism discipline in the set. Fixed-seed CPU-baked tileable FBM (no Math.random, retained Float32 sources), cloud offsets INTEGRATED from Δ(world.state.clock) rather than closed-form — correctly reasoned: 'weather re-rolls… change windDir discontinuously and would teleport every shadow; integration just bends the streamlines'. The sim-clock/wall-clock split (world-anchored clouds freeze on pause, screen-space precip stays wall-clock) is the right pause-semantics ruling for the whole stack and should become the standing rule. The shared-Float32-arrays parity scheme (fogVisAt sampling the SAME arrays the GPU textures were uploaded from, gated by scratch-atmo-parity.ts asserting <0.02 error on 64 probes) is the only honest answer to 2D/GL fog coherence within the decided architecture. The 128² local-valley-floor min-field is the trick that makes pooling real on a floor that rises 1550→2000 m — a fixed MSL ceiling provably fails. Legibility caps as named constants (SYMBOLOGY_FOG_FLOOR=0.55, CLOUD_DENSE_MAX=0.45) encode the legibility law in code. Save compat handled honestly: uWet/uSnowAcc explicitly NOT persisted, 'flagged in open questions rather than silently widening the save schema'. All atmosphere textures 8-bit UNORM — SwiftShader-safe by construction. Render-side integrator keyed to World identity, reset on swap — same pattern as the bake WeakMap; reads World, never writes.

**Weaknesses:** Its splice contract assumes things the terrain core forbids ('extend TerrainEnv, never the shader plumbing') — an unowned seam that will deadlock implementation if not assigned. Its fog ownership directly contradicts the art-seam axis (GL fog + sprite alpha fade vs top-canvas occluding blobs) — both cannot ship. fogVisAt cost is undercounted at the worst case: it claims ~100-300 calls/frame but the decoration cap is 1800 (per P4's own perf section); each call does an elevAt plus two bilinears — needs a fast early-out when fogTopAdd≈0 (the overwhelmingly common state). Snow ownership (baked 68% cap vs in-shader) is left open and interacts with P1's pixel-for-pixel albedo argument. The cloud-deck sun-parallax (off weather.ceiling) is admitted by its own open question to be possibly invisible top-down — candidate cut. Minor: weather.ceiling AGL-vs-MSL is unresolved and load-bearing for both uCloudAlt and Fog depth.

**Must fix:**
- Settle the shader-extension seam with the terrain core BEFORE implementation: this axis ships a named GLSL chunk (atmosphere.glsl.ts) composed at a defined splice point in the terrain fragment shader, with Phase-B neutral defaults reproducing the baked look (uHazeK=0.105, warm/cool 0.08/0.13) gated by a near-zero screenshot diff
- Win the fog-ownership conflict explicitly (this design over P4's top-canvas blobs) and add an early-out so fogVisAt costs ~0 when fog depth is ~0, sized against the real 1800-item decoration cap, not the assumed 100-300
- Enforce the single-fold luminance rule jointly with the sky axis: the absorbed GL veil (uVeilA) and live haze must be chroma-budgeted so terrain mean luma still tracks ambientLight() — extend the sky probe to assert it across the 6 weather states

## Art-seam (sprite shadows, decoration/grade coherence, draw order, hero assets, ART_BIBLE contract) — score 7
**Strengths:** Three genuinely load-bearing finds. (1) The latent rotated-statics bug: HESCO/ECP segments drawn with rot (draw.ts:404,445) already point their baked 'SE' shadows the wrong way around the ring — projecting in world axes after opts.rot fixes a real defect, not a cosmetic. (2) The sticker problem: the night wash runs inside drawTerrain BEFORE decoration/villages/COP (verified against topo.ts:587-592 call order), so sprites render full-bright at night today — over a GL-graded terrain that becomes glaring; the world-dressing-under-grade / units-ink-FX-above-grade re-cut is the correct codification of the legibility law. (3) The QA design is excellent harness thinking: Phase P1 renders runtime shadows at the FIXED legacy NW angle so the SVG strip is verifiable as a pure screenshot-diff against baseline, independent of all GL work; and the build-time cross-validation (castHeight ⟺ data-cast-shadow group, mismatch = build error) honors the generated-files invariant — sources edited, manifest regenerated, both failure modes made into compile-time errors. The relief-inversion flag (noon sun due south on a north-up map) with a concrete tunable fix (25-35% NW form light) is a real cartographic concern the other axes missed. Blur confined to bake time, shadows gated on shadowAlpha — deterministic and harness-safe.

**Weaknesses:** Weakest on my rubric's drift hazards. It ships a THIRD sun model (lib/render/sun.ts, δ=+15°, solar noon 12:15) with constants that disagree with both other proposals — 12:15 noon is unjustified against the symmetric gameplay ramps. Worse, gradeFor() re-derives night from sun elevation ('night = 1 − clamp01((el + 6°)/12°)') instead of being parameterized by solarLight×weatherLightMult — exactly the silent-drift hazard P2 explicitly rejected; its night could disagree with isNight()/ambientLight gates. Its binding 'GL parity rule' — GL grade 'must be mix(color, uGradeTint, uGradeAmt)… NOT a multiply' — would gut P2's exposure/white-balance/saturation/lift grade and is over-constrained; parity belongs in calibration screenshots, not in mandating the weaker math on both canvases. Its fog ownership (occluding blobs on the top canvas, 'gl-core keeps only surface haze') loses to P3's parity-tested shared-field design; the stated objection (qalats poking through fog banks) is precisely what P3's fogVisAt sprite-alpha fade answers. Perf mitigation 'shadows are fully OFF at night' silently conflicts with P2's moon shadows — needs one decision. One typo-grade slip ('separate全-shadow pass') suggests lighter review than the others.

**Must fix:**
- Delete lib/render/sun.ts as an independent model: sunState/spriteShadow must be consumed from the unified sky module (δ=+21°, noon 12:00, P2's verified table); sprite shadow alpha keyed to max(sunIntensity, moonFactor) so sprites cast under P2's moon — then re-measure the forest worst case, since 'shadows off all night' no longer subsidizes the perf budget
- Replace gradeFor()'s elevation-derived night with the nightFactor ≡ 1 − clamp01(solar × weatherLightMult) identity (solarLight passed in) — the current form re-implements the sacred curve by proxy and will drift from isNight()/ambientLight semantics
- Drop the 'GL grade must be the identical alpha-mix' parity mandate; replace with the shared keyframe table + the proposal's own calibration-screenshot gate (06:00/12:00/17:30/22:00 on both canvases) — the GL terrain keeps P2's full exposure/WB/saturation grade
- Cede fog to the atmosphere axis: kill the drawWeather pooling-blob upgrade; wire decoration/village/COP draw alphas to fogVisAt with P3's per-class floors instead (keep this axis's draw-order move of precip above structures)

## Cross-axis conflicts
- **P1 (gl/sun.ts, δ=+20°, noon 12:00) vs P2 (atmosphere.ts, δ=+21°, noon 12:00) vs P4 (render/sun.ts, δ=+15°, noon 12:15)**: Three independent sun models with three declinations and two solar noons. Sprite shadows pointing differently than ridge shadows — P4's own words: 'worse than no shadows' — is the failure mode if any two survive.
  - resolution: Exactly one module (P2's, renamed lib/render/sky.ts to avoid the atmosphere.ts name collision with P3): δ=+21°, noon 12:00, with the verified consistency table and scratch-sun-probe. P1's TerrainGL consumes sunDir/keyDir as uniforms; P4's sprite projection consumes spriteShadow. The probe asserting sun-below-horizon ⇔ solarLight-at-starlight across 24 h × 6 weathers is the merge gate.
- **P2 (in-shader exposure/WB/saturation/lift grade + source-atop spriteTint table) vs P4 (gradeFor() + binding rule that GL grade be the identical alpha-mix) vs P1 (night wash retained, u_nightMix hook reserved)**: Two grade owners with different math, and P4's parity mandate would forbid P2's richer GL grade outright. P4's gradeFor also re-derives night from sun elevation, bypassing the sacred curve. Separately, P1 keeps the navy wash through Phase 3 while P2/P4 both delete it.
  - resolution: P2 owns the grade: one altitude-keyed table, GL side gets the full grade() shader, 2D side gets the source-atop spriteTint as the mid-tone approximation; coherence is verified by P4's calibration-screenshot gate, not by weakening the GL math. nightFactor comes from the 1 − clamp01(solar×weatherLightMult) identity everywhere. Sequencing per P1: wash survives until the Phase-3 night relight lands, then dies in one commit alongside the grade seam; P4's seam placement (world dressing under, units/ink/FX above, drawCopNightLights split out and drawn after) is the ordering that ships.
- **P3 (GL pooled fog + shared fogVisAt fading 2D sprite alphas) vs P4 (occluding fog blobs on the top canvas; 'gl-core keeps only surface haze')**: Directly contradictory owners for valley fog. P4 argues GL fog under the sprite layer makes 'qalats and trees poke through fog banks'; P3 answers by fading the sprites themselves with exact parity to the GL field.
  - resolution: P3 wins. Shared Float32 arrays + the parity harness (<0.02 transmittance error) is verifiable headlessly; P4's blob upgrade keeps a hand-drawn placeholder alive that P3 explicitly supersedes. P4's legitimate concern is covered because a sprite faded by the same transmittance the terrain shows IS the occlusion, with P3's named floors (symbology ≥0.55) preserving the tactical read. Graft from P4: precip draws above structures (its step-6 reorder).
- **P1 ('axes extend TerrainEnv, never the shader plumbing'; u_cloud = one tap at uv+offset) vs P3 (atmosphere.glsl.ts chunk: elev-parallax cloudShadow, cloudMottle on ambient, fogT — 'spliced into the terrain shader by the GL-core axis')**: P1's uniforms-only extension contract cannot express P3's terms (cloud parallax needs per-pixel elev; mottle multiplies ambient, not vis; fog needs heightAt + exp). An unowned seam — P3 assumes a splice P1 forbids.
  - resolution: Amend P1's contract: the atmosphere axis gets a named GLSL splice point (its chunk functions compiled into the terrain fragment shader, with Phase-B neutral defaults gated by near-zero screenshot diff vs the baked look); the grade axis stays uniforms-only as P1 designed. P1's unit-3 1×1-white reservation remains the pre-splice default so the core ships before atmosphere lands.
- **P1 (shadow pass: 1024² R8, exponential stride spanning 2.56 km, >0.25° + ≤4 Hz, no moon — skip when alt<0) vs P2 (48×10 m = 480 m linear march, >0.5°, sun↔moon key-swap, one-rebake-per-2-frames cap; claims policy ownership of shadow-pass.ts)**: Two implementations of the same FBO pass with incompatible reach and no agreed file owner — P2's own open question. P2's 480 m reach truncates real shadows (780 m ridge at 9° alt throws ~4.9 km); P1's pass can't do moonlight at all.
  - resolution: One writer: P1 owns the pass file and the march (exponential stride, whole-map reach, 1.5 m bias). P2 owns the policy and supplies keyDir: key-swap to the moon at night (swap occurs while both intensities ≈0, invisible), rebake threshold 0.25-0.5° (tune in Phase 3), per-2-frame cap under extreme warp. P1's alt<0 skip is replaced by 'bake whatever keyDir says'.
- **P1 (per-fragment in-shader normals from the height texture; normal texture rejected as 'a second source of truth') vs P2 (CPU-precomputed RGBA8 normal texture) vs P4 (true central-difference GLSL sketch)**: Three normal conventions. Verified against topo.ts:156-165: the bake uses a FORWARD difference — P1 cites it correctly, P2 and P4 both miscite it as central-difference, and P4's l/r/u/d sketch would shift relief half a cell relative to the contours drawn from the same elev array.
  - resolution: P1's in-shader forward-difference normals from the bit-faithful heightAt, full stop. P2's lighting combine consumes normalAt() instead of uNormalTex (1 MB and a bake step deleted); P4's GLSL consistency snippets are documentation only and must be corrected to the forward difference.
- **P2 (single-fold rule: exposure carries weatherLightMult exactly once) vs P3 (absorbed GL veil uVeilA + live weather-scaled haze add their own weather-dependent terms)**: Weather luminance can be double-counted across the two modules — P2's own named risk ('overcast nights twice as dark as the gameplay model says') now spans an axis boundary where neither proposal alone can see it.
  - resolution: Luminance budget owned by P2's exposure alone; P3's veil and haze are chroma/contrast shifts with capped luma impact (VEIL_A_MAX=0.18 stands). Extend P2's scratch probe to a joint gate: rendered terrain mean luma must track ambientLight() within tolerance across 24 h × 6 weathers with the atmosphere chunk enabled.
- **P3 (clouds/fog advect on sim clock, frozen at pause; precip stays wall-clock) vs P1/P2 (shadow rebakes throttled on wall-clock ms) vs P4 (shadows 'fully OFF at night' as a perf saving, vs P2's moon shadows)**: Pause/clock semantics and the night perf budget are decided differently per axis: if sprites cast moon shadows (P2's keyDir), P4's '~35-40% of clock time' shadow-free perf subsidy disappears; wall-clock rebake throttles must not leak into output determinism.
  - resolution: Adopt P3's split as the standing rule: world-anchored phenomena follow world time (pause = still photo), screen-space ephemera follow wall clock. Wall-clock appears only in rate LIMITERS (P1's ≤4 Hz, P2's per-2-frame cap) whose output is angle-threshold-determined — at a pinned clock the frame is reproducible, which keeps --hour screenshot goldens valid. Sprite shadows follow keyDir (moon included) at P2's alpha formula; P4 re-measures the forest worst case at moonlit night before P2's relight phase merges.

## Overall
No proposal couples render state into the sim — all four read the live World and write nothing back; the closest calls are P2's import of the pure exported weatherLightMult (legal, and actively good: single source of truth) and P3's render-side integrators keyed to World identity (the established bake-WeakMap pattern; correctly NOT persisted, so the save schema is untouched). Gameplay-light semantics survive in all four, but only P2 makes that survival mechanically impossible to break.

SPINE: P1, the terrain core. It is the most architecturally disciplined design — bit-faithful heightAt, unlit-albedo hybrid, byte-identical 2D fallback preserving the entire headless-harness ecology, one draw call, phased with parity gates. Build P1's Phases 0-1 first (refactor + GL blit parity) exactly as written.

GRAFT ONTO THE SPINE, in dependency order: (1) P2's sky module replaces P1's sun.ts and P4's sun.ts as the single source of sun/moon/grade truth — δ=+21°, noon 12:00, the nightFactor identity, the consistency probe; P1's Phase 2 consumes its uniforms. (2) P1's shadow pass keeps its exponential whole-map march but takes P2's keyDir (moon key-swap) and refresh policy; one writer: terrain axis owns the file, sky axis owns the constants. (3) P4's SVG strip + runtime sprite shadows land in parallel at the fixed legacy NW angle (pure screenshot-diff QA), switching to live sunState only in the same PR as the WorldView reorder, grade seam, and night relight — the wash dies in that one commit, per P1's conservatism. (4) P3 splices last through a named GLSL seam P1 must open (amend the 'never the shader plumbing' contract for the atmosphere chunk only; grade stays uniforms-only), with Phase-B neutral defaults gated by near-zero diff, then clouds, then fog with fogVisAt wired into P4's draw paths.

CUT: P1's gl/sun.ts and δ=+20° (lose to P2's verified table); P2's RGBA8 normal texture and 480 m shadow march (lose to P1 on single-source-of-truth and geometry — 480 m truncates real ridge shadows); P4's entire sun/grade model, its GL-grade-must-be-alpha-mix parity mandate (replaced by the shared table + calibration screenshots), and its top-canvas fog blobs (lose to P3's parity-tested shared field); P3's cloud sun-parallax if Phase-C screenshots show it invisible top-down (its own open question names it a clean delete); P2's SkyState fields that overlap P3's ownership (cloudShadowDensity, fogColor) become inputs P3 computes, not sky outputs.
